Convolution from Impulse Responses

An LTI system is completely described by its impulse response h(t). To see why, cut the input x(t) into narrow pulses of width Δ. Each pulse is a scaled, shifted copy of one basic pulse, so by linearity and time invariance its output is the same scaled, shifted copy of the basic pulse response. Adding up the responses to all the pulses gives the output. As Δ → 0, each pulse acts like an impulse of area x(kΔ)Δ, and the sum becomes the convolution integral:

y(t) ≈ Σ x(kΔ) h(t-kΔ) Δ → y(t) = ∫ x(τ) h(t-τ) dτ

Try it: press Play to add the pulses one at a time and watch each response join the running sum. Then move Δ to the left to make the pulses narrower: the staircase hugs x(t) more closely, and the sum of the responses (blue) lands on the exact output (dashed).
