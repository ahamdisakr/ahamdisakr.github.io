Amplitude Modulation

Multiplying a message m(t) by a carrier cos(2πfct) moves its spectrum to ±fc: this is the modulation property, m(t) cos(2πfct) ↔ ½[M(f − fc) + M(f + fc)]. The receiver multiplies by the carrier again, which puts one copy back at 0 and others at ±2fc, and a low-pass filter keeps the one at 0. In DSB-SC (double sideband, suppressed carrier) the transmitted signal is m(t) cos(2πfct); in AM a carrier is added, (1 + μ m(t)) cos(2πfct), so the envelope itself follows the message.

Try it: start with DSB-SC: the spectrum of s(t) is the message spectrum moved to ±fc at half height, and the receiver output lies on m(t). Press Sweep to turn the receiver's phase error φ: the output shrinks by cos φ, vanishes at 90° and comes back inverted. Switch to AM: a carrier line appears at ±fc and the envelope of s(t) traces 1 + μm(t). Choose the envelope detector: it needs no carrier at the receiver and still recovers the message. Raise μ above 1: the envelope folds over (overmodulation) and the detector output is distorted, while the coherent receiver still recovers the message. Switch back to DSB-SC with the envelope detector: it gives |m(t)|, which is why DSB-SC needs a coherent receiver and AM pays for a carrier. Finally, choose the two tones, raise fm and lower fc until the two copies of the spectrum overlap. To see the sidebands closely, zoom into a spectrum (Ctrl + scroll or pinch, drag to pan, double-click to reset).

Predict, then check

A message has its highest frequency at W. What range of frequencies does the DSB-SC signal occupy?

From fc − W to fc + W (and the mirror image at negative frequencies): a bandwidth of 2W, twice the message bandwidth, since both sidebands are sent.

With DSB-SC and a receiver phase error of 90°, what comes out?

Nothing. The output is m(t) cos φ, and cos 90° = 0: the receiver's carrier is in quadrature with the transmitter's, so the product has no low-frequency part. That is why coherent receivers must track the carrier phase.

The receiver multiplies by 2cos(2πfct + φ) and keeps |f| < fc.
An ideal envelope detector follows the peaks of s(t): its output is |1 + μm(t)| for AM and |m(t)| for DSB-SC. It needs no carrier at the receiver.
