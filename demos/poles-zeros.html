Poles, Zeros and Frequency Response

A rational transfer function is fixed, up to a gain K, by its zeros zi and poles pi. In continuous time the frequency response is H(s) on the jω axis; in discrete time it is H(z) on the unit circle z = ejΩ. Each factor is a vector from a pole or zero to that point, so

|H(jω)| = K ∏|jω − zi| / ∏|jω − pi|, ∠H(jω) = Σ∠(jω − zi) − Σ∠(jω − pi)

Try it: with the resonance preset, press Sweep and watch the dashed vectors: as the point passes the pole near 4j, its vector becomes short and |H| peaks. Drag that pole toward the jω axis: the peak gets taller and narrower and h(t) rings longer. Drag it into the right half-plane and h(t) grows without bound. Choose the notch preset: a zero on the axis makes |H| exactly zero at that frequency. Then switch to the z-plane, where the unit circle plays the role of the jω axis and stable poles lie inside the circle. Finally, choose the Bode view in the s-plane: with the first-order low-pass, the magnitude falls 20 dB per decade above the pole, and the second-order high-pass rises 40 dB per decade below its poles, 20 dB for each zero at the origin.

Predict, then check

With poles at −σ ± 4j, what happens to the peak of |H| and to h(t) as σ shrinks toward 0?

The peak near ω = 4 rad/s gets taller and narrower (its width is about 2σ), and h(t) ∝ e−σt sin(4t) rings longer, since it decays with time constant 1/σ.

In the z-plane, a single pole at z = −0.9: low-pass or high-pass? What does h[n] look like?

High-pass. The pole is closest to z = −1 = ejπ, the highest frequency Ω = π, so |H| peaks there. The impulse response h[n] ∝ (−0.9)n alternates in sign as it decays.
